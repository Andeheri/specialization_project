\begin{abstract}
This thesis investigates modelling, parameter estimation, and control strategies for a tendon-driven hand exoskeleton intended to assist hand function in spinal-cord-injured patients. The exoskeleton is actuated by brushed DC motors through Bowden-tube cable transmissions, introducing uncertain dynamics, friction, and limited sensing at the hand. The work focuses on a single finger as a representative subsystem of the full 6-DOF exoskeleton.

A planar three-link finger model was derived using the Euler-Lagrange equations, including joint stiffness and damping. A brushed DC motor model was also developed, and relevant motor parameters were estimated from datasheet values. For parameters not available from the datasheet, a gradient-based identification method was formulated and evaluated in simulation. The motor and finger models were then connected through a cable constraint, where the resulting Lagrange multipliers were interpreted as cable tensions.

A discrete-time current controller was designed using proportional feedback with a feedforward term to compensate for back-EMF. Simulations showed that the controller can track the armature current rapidly compared to the mechanical dynamics, supporting the use of armature current as an effective control input for torque and position control. For force control, simulations indicated that Bowden-tube friction causes steady-state force errors when only motor-side information is used, while integrated fingertip force sensors allow the desired applied force to be reached more reliably. For position control, a model reference adaptive control scheme was designed using the motor angle as a proxy for fingertip position. The adaptive controller improved tracking over time, but the adaptive gains were observed to grow slowly due to nonlinearities, saturation, and modelling assumptions. The results therefore suggest that the adaptive scheme is most suitable as an automatic tuning method for finding fixed controller gains.

Overall, the proposed controllers provide a simulation-based proof of concept for future implementation on the physical exoskeleton. Experimental validation is required to assess the effects of cable elasticity, Bowden-tube friction, whiffle-tree geometry, sensor limitations, and user-dependent hand dynamics.
\end{abstract}
